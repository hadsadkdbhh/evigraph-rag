\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation systems often treat retrieved passages as usable evidence, even when the retrieved context is incomplete, noisy, or numerically under-specified.
We formulate \emph{Evidence State Optimization} (ESO): instead of consuming a retrieved top-\(K\) prefix, a RAG system searches over subsets of evidence units for a compact, executable, and verifiable support state.
EviGraph-RAG instantiates ESO by building a query-local evidence graph, selecting a Minimal Reliable Support Subgraph, executing table-oriented numerical operations, and verifying generated claims against cited support.
On deterministic FinQA-300 and FinQA-600 subsets, plus 50- and 100-example public TAT-QA portability pilots, EviGraph-RAG is evaluated against direct RAG, retrieve-then-program, utility-only, retrieval, external LLM, and component-ablation controls.
The results show a consistent mechanism: retrieval exposure alone does not reliably become answer accuracy, while verifier-guided evidence-state selection improves the use of heterogeneous candidates and reveals when high exact match lacks cited numerical support.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large language models can fluently answer questions over retrieved documents, but numerical QA often requires a stricter notion of evidence than lexical relevance.
A passage may mention the right company and year while omitting the denominator needed for a percentage calculation; a table row may contain the right number but the wrong period or financial quantity.
This paper asks whether retrieval outputs should be treated as proposals in an \emph{evidence state space}, rather than as final context.

We formulate \emph{Evidence State Optimization} (ESO): given retrieved candidates, find a selected state of evidence units that is sufficient, low-risk, executable, and verifiable.
Each retrieved item becomes a node with utility, risk, cost, and provenance signals.
The system searches over evidence states, executes local table operations, and verifies answer traces against citations.

We make three contributions.
First, we define ESO as a RAG problem in which the search object is an evidence state \(S\subseteq V_q\), not a document, chunk, path, or retrieved prefix.
Second, we define a \emph{Minimal Reliable Support Subgraph} (MRSG) objective combining coverage, source/temporal/operation risk, connectivity, executability, and verifier support; a monotone submodular relaxation gives a standard greedy approximation guarantee under a cardinality budget.
Third, we instantiate ESO as EviGraph-RAG, a reproducible numerical QA pipeline with table-operation execution, verifier diagnostics, failure-driven repair, and retrieval-portfolio selection.

The experiments are designed to test mechanism rather than claim a leaderboard result.
On FinQA-300, EviGraph reaches 0.670/0.523/0.670 exact match under oracle-document, open BM25, and source-rerank settings, with answer-support rates of 0.850/0.853/0.850.
A GPT-5.4 Direct RAG baseline matches or exceeds exact match in some settings, but its answer-support rates are much lower (0.343/0.273/0.340), showing why exact match alone is insufficient for grounded numerical RAG.
On the larger FinQA-600 stress subset, guarded retrieval-portfolio selection raises open-retrieval exact match from 0.377 to 0.407 with 18 paired wins and no paired losses against BM25.
TAT-QA-50/100 pilots provide a conservative portability check on a second public financial table/text QA format.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Retrieval-Augmented Generation.}
Retrieval-augmented generation combines parametric generation with retrieved non-parametric memory, making retrieval quality and context selection central to answer faithfulness~\cite{lewis2020retrieval}.
Dense Passage Retrieval further shows that learned retrievers can replace or complement sparse retrieval for open-domain QA~\cite{karpukhin2020dense}.
EviGraph-RAG is complementary: it treats retrieved candidates as an intermediate evidence state that must be selected, risk-scored, and verified before generation.

\paragraph{Graph-Structured and Multimodal RAG.}
Recent graph-based RAG systems use structured relations to move beyond flat top-\(K\) retrieval.
MG\({}^2\)-RAG and ReAG build multimodal retrieval pipelines with graph or critic-based filtering~\cite{dai2026mg2rag,compagnoni2026reag}.
Graph-R1 and EvoGraph-R1 cast graph retrieval as an agentic or MDP-style process over graph actions~\cite{luo2026graphr1,lin2026evographr1}.
Our setting is narrower: EviGraph-RAG does not build a persistent self-evolving multimodal graph or learn an RL policy.
It builds a query-local evidence graph over financial text, tables, derived operations, and verifier judgments, then asks whether that graph improves executable grounding and support diagnostics.

\paragraph{Table and Financial QA.}
Financial QA benchmarks such as FinQA and TAT-QA require numerical reasoning over tables and surrounding text~\cite{chen2021finqa,zhu2021tatqa}.
These tasks expose a weakness of passage-only RAG: the relevant context may be split across row labels, year columns, prose definitions, and derived operations.
They therefore provide a natural setting for testing whether evidence-state selection improves source provenance, table grounding, and auditable calculation traces.

\paragraph{Verifiable and Tool-Augmented QA.}
Tool-augmented reasoning frameworks such as ReAct interleave reasoning traces with external actions~\cite{yao2023react}.
Recent process-level RAG work similarly evaluates trajectory quality, search coordination, or capability transfer rather than final answers alone~\cite{li2026mcsearch,kotoge2026dgpo}.
EviGraph-RAG adopts this process view for numerical QA: table operations are executed locally, cited values are checked against the support graph, and verifier diagnostics are reported separately from exact-match accuracy.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Problem Setting and Evidence Objects}

Given a query \(q\), a retriever returns a candidate pool \(C_q=\{c_1,\ldots,c_m\}\) consisting of passages, table fragments, source-local context, and retrieval-portfolio alternatives.
Standard RAG passes this pool, or a top-\(K\) prefix, directly to a reader.
EviGraph-RAG inserts an explicit evidence-state layer between retrieval and generation.

\begin{definition}[Evidence Unit]
An evidence unit \(v\) is an atomic object that may participate in answering \(q\): a text span, table row or column fragment, source cue, parsed numeric value, operation candidate, or verifier judgment.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Candidate Evidence Graph]
The candidate evidence graph for \(q\) is \(G_q=(V_q,E_q)\), where \(V_q\) is the set of evidence units induced by \(C_q\).
Edges in \(E_q\) encode support, derivation, source provenance, temporal adjacency, row/column alignment, and potential conflict relations.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Evidence State and Support Subgraph]
An evidence state is a subset \(S_t\subseteq V_q\) at controller step \(t\).
It becomes a reliable support subgraph only when the induced graph \(G_q[S_t]\) contains enough cited source values, executable operations, and verifier checks to support the final answer trace.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Evidence State Space]
The evidence state space is \(\Omega_q=(\mathcal{S}_q,\mathcal{T}_q)\), where \(\mathcal{S}_q\subseteq 2^{V_q}\) is the set of candidate evidence states satisfying the system's budget and type constraints, and \(\mathcal{T}_q\) is a set of state transitions such as expansion, merge, prune, rank, and repair.
\end{definition}

These definitions separate \emph{retrieved context} from \emph{evidence}: a retrieved node can be relevant but still unusable if it lacks the denominator, names the wrong period, comes from a conflicting source, or cannot support the executed operation.
The search object is therefore a state \(S\in\mathcal{S}_q\), not a document, chunk, or path.

\subsection{Evidence State Optimization}

We define Evidence State Optimization (ESO) as the problem of searching \(\Omega_q\) for a state that is useful, low-risk, executable, and verifiable.
Let \(\operatorname{Cov}(S,q)\) denote coverage of required query aspects, \(\operatorname{Risk}(S,q)\) aggregate source, temporal, operation, and conflict risks, \(\operatorname{Conn}(G_q[S])\) indicate whether the induced support graph is connected enough for the required reasoning chain, \(\operatorname{Exec}(S,q)\) indicate whether the required numerical operation can be executed over cited operands, and \(\operatorname{Ver}(S,q)\) indicate whether the answer trace passes verifier checks.
The ideal Minimal Reliable Support Subgraph (MRSG) is:
\[
S^\star =
\arg\min_{S\in\mathcal{S}_q} |S|
\quad
\text{s.t.}\quad
\operatorname{Cov}(S,q)\ge \tau,\;
\operatorname{Risk}(S,q)\le \delta,\;
\operatorname{Conn}(G_q[S])=1,\;
\operatorname{Exec}(S,q)=1,\;
\operatorname{Ver}(S,q)=1.
\]
Thus MRSG is the smallest evidence state that covers the needed information while satisfying graph, operation, and verification constraints.
Exact constrained search over \(\mathcal{S}_q\) is combinatorial, so EviGraph-RAG uses a verifier-guided relaxation: greedily construct high-coverage, low-risk states under a node budget, execute candidate operations locally, and repair only diagnosed source, period, row, denominator, or operation failures.

\subsection{Evidence-State Controller}

The controller is a transparent sequence of operations rather than a hidden reader prompt.
It transforms candidate states through four modules.
\textbf{Expansion} adds source-local context, table rows, operation candidates, and portfolio alternatives.
\textbf{Pruning} removes source-inconsistent, period-incomplete, row-ungrounded, or operation-incompatible nodes.
\textbf{Ranking} builds a compact state using the utility-risk objective.
\textbf{Repair} revisits operands, years, row labels, or retrieval exposure when verifier diagnostics identify a concrete failure.

Formally, the controller maintains \(S_t\subseteq V_q\) and applies transitions
\[
S_{t+1} = \pi_\theta(S_t, G_q, q, \mathcal{D}_t),
\]
where \(\mathcal{D}_t\) is the diagnostic state containing citation, arithmetic, row-grounding, operation-type, source-consistency, and period-coverage checks.
In the reported implementation, \(\pi_\theta\) is deterministic and verifier-guided; the formulation leaves room for learned or policy-optimized controllers, but the present paper reports the reproducible deterministic variant.

Operationally, the implementation is a bounded search: initialize states from retrieval candidates, expand with source-local evidence and operation candidates, rank by utility-risk coverage, prune hard source/period/executability failures, and repair failed states using verifier diagnostics.
All transitions are recorded as graph actions, executed calculations, and verifier outputs.

\subsection{Subgraph Selection Objective}

Let the candidate evidence graph be \(G=(V,E)\), where each node \(v\in V\) is an evidence unit and each edge \(e\in E\) records support, derivation, source provenance, temporal adjacency, row/column alignment, or potential conflict.
For a question \(q\), let \(\mathcal{A}_q\) denote a finite set of required evidence aspects, such as entity overlap, year coverage, table-row relevance, denominator availability, and operation cues.
Each node has an aspect utility \(u_{v,a}\in[0,1]\) for \(a\in\mathcal{A}_q\).
Source, structural, temporal, operation, and conflict risks are propagated from \(G\) and the verifier diagnostics into node-level scores \(r^{\mathrm{src}}_v,r^{\mathrm{str}}_v,r^{\mathrm{tmp}}_v,r^{\mathrm{op}}_v,r^{\mathrm{conf}}_v\in[0,1]\).
We define a risk discount
\[
\rho_v=\exp\left(
-\lambda_{\mathrm{src}}r^{\mathrm{src}}_v
-\lambda_{\mathrm{str}}r^{\mathrm{str}}_v
-\lambda_{\mathrm{tmp}}r^{\mathrm{tmp}}_v
-\lambda_{\mathrm{op}}r^{\mathrm{op}}_v
-\lambda_{\mathrm{conf}}r^{\mathrm{conf}}_v
\right)
\]
and risk-adjusted aspect coverage \(p_{v,a}=\rho_v u_{v,a}\).
The utility of a selected support subgraph \(S\subseteq V\) is
\[
f(S)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}_q} w_a
\left(1-\prod_{v\in S}(1-p_{v,a})\right),
\]
where \(w_a\ge 0\) is the importance of aspect \(a\).
This objective rewards complementary evidence while discounting nodes whose graph and verifier context indicates source, structural, temporal, operation, or conflict risk.

\begin{lemma}
The function \(f(S)\) is monotone submodular over \(V\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Consider adding node \(v\notin S\).
The marginal gain is
\[
\Delta_v(S)=f(S\cup\{v\})-f(S)
=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}_q} w_a p_{v,a}
\prod_{x\in S}(1-p_{x,a}).
\]
Since \(p_{x,a}\in[0,1]\), for any \(S\subseteq T\subseteq V\setminus\{v\}\),
\[
\prod_{x\in S}(1-p_{x,a})\ge
\prod_{x\in T}(1-p_{x,a}).
\]
Multiplying by nonnegative \(w_a p_{v,a}\) and summing over \(a\) gives
\[
\Delta_v(S)\ge \Delta_v(T).
\]
Thus \(f\) has diminishing marginal returns and is submodular.
The same marginal expression is nonnegative, so \(f\) is monotone.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
Under the cardinality constraint \(|S|\le K\), greedy subgraph selection achieves a \((1-1/e)\)-approximation to the optimal risk-adjusted support set.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The previous lemma establishes that \(f\) is nonnegative, monotone, and submodular.
Therefore the classical result of Nemhauser, Wolsey, and Fisher~\cite{nemhauser1978analysis} applies: repeatedly adding the node with largest marginal gain returns a set \(S_{\mathrm{greedy}}\) satisfying
\[
f(S_{\mathrm{greedy}})\ge (1-1/e)\max_{|S|\le K} f(S).
\]
In EviGraph-RAG, this justifies greedy node pruning as a budgeted approximation to selecting a compact, risk-adjusted evidence subgraph rather than an arbitrary top-\(K\) retrieval prefix.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Table Operation Executor}

For numerical QA, the system executes table operations including row/column selection, sum, difference, ratio, percent change, and average.
Each executed operation returns both a numeric value and a human-readable expression used in the final answer trace.
When deterministic rules fail, an optional LLM numeric planner can propose a structured operation plan; EviGraph-RAG validates that operands occur in cited context and executes locally rather than accepting free-form numeric text.
The local planner also recovers operations from inline serialized table rows when chunking truncates the Markdown table.

\subsection{Claim Verification}

Generated answers are verified against the selected support graph.
The verifier checks citation presence, source-number consistency, calculation-result consistency, operation-type consistency, and whether calculation row labels are lexically grounded in the query.

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Datasets}

\textbf{FinQA.} We use a deterministic 300-example validation subset from \texttt{dreamerdeo/finqa}, preserving source-document metadata for oracle-document and source-rerank analysis.
The corpus serializes source pre-text, tables, and post-text to Markdown and excludes gold answers and gold evidence annotations from retrieval.

\textbf{TAT-QA pilot.} We construct 50- and 100-example arithmetic pilots from the public TAT-QA development set using a fixed seed.
Gold derivations are excluded from the retrieval corpus.
This pilot tests portability on a second public financial table/text QA format; it is not a full TAT-QA benchmark claim.

\textbf{Stress Suite.} We also maintain a synthetic stress suite with official reports and distracting forecast/draft/press evidence.
This suite is used only for controlled mechanism debugging.

\subsection{Retrieval Settings}

\textbf{Oracle-doc} constrains retrieval to the gold \texttt{source\_doc} and expands full source context for numerical evidence.
\textbf{Open BM25} retrieves from the full corpus without source metadata and is treated as a stress setting for retrieval exposure.
\textbf{Neural dense and neural hybrid} use sentence-transformer embeddings, with the hybrid setting combining embedding cosine similarity, normalized BM25, and lightweight numeric/table overlap features; these runs are reported separately from lexical BM25.
\textbf{BM25 + source rerank} retrieves broadly and softly promotes candidates whose source matches the known \texttt{source\_doc}; this is an analysis setting, not a deployable open-retrieval claim.

\subsection{Baselines and Ablations}

We report a baseline ladder designed to answer the main reviewer question: whether EviGraph-RAG improves over simpler RAG and tool-use alternatives.
\textbf{Direct RAG} uses retrieval-order evidence without the local operation planner.
\textbf{LLM Direct RAG} sends the same retrieval-order evidence to an external reader without EviGraph planning or support selection.
\textbf{Retrieve-then-program} uses the same retrieval-order evidence with the local program planner but without evidence-graph selection.
\textbf{Full context} passes all retrieved candidates to the same answer generator.
\textbf{Utility-only} selects evidence using utility without risk-aware graph control.
We also report ablations that remove planner, verifier-grounded rejection, or support-subgraph extraction where applicable.

The v28 component-ablation run shows that the operation planner contributes 7.3 exact-match points under oracle-document retrieval, 6.0 points under open BM25, and 7.7 points under source reranking over the no-planner ablation.
Risk-aware graph selection is most visible against the utility-only selector: full EviGraph improves exact match by 3.3 points under oracle-document retrieval, 11.7 points under open BM25, and 10.3 points under source reranking.
The strongest external reader baseline, GPT-5.4 Direct RAG over the same retrieval-order context, reaches 0.693 exact match under oracle-document retrieval, 0.523 under open BM25, and 0.690 under source reranking, but only 0.343, 0.273, and 0.340 answer support.
Thus Table~\ref{tab:baseline-ladder} should be read as both an accuracy table and a grounding table: higher exact match does not necessarily mean better cited numerical support.

\subsection{Open-Retrieval Baseline Stress Test}

We additionally compare open retrieval variants while keeping the local program planner and verifier fixed.
Open BM25 remains the strongest non-API retrieval control on FinQA-300 after failure-driven repairs, reaching 0.523 exact match.
The 600-example stress subset is harder: BM25 top-\(8\) reaches 0.377, while neural-hybrid top-\(16\) improves source hit@16 (0.927 versus 0.905 for BM25 top-\(16\)) without improving final exact match by itself.
The paired sets are complementary, however, with an oracle portfolio union of 248/600 (0.413).
A no-gold retrieval-portfolio selector that switches from BM25 to neural hybrid using fallback evidence coverage, executable numeric calculations, and verifier-supported calculation-refinement signals reaches 0.407 exact match, improving over the BM25 top-8 v43 result of 0.377 with 74 switches, 18 paired wins, and no paired losses after adding a two-year coverage guard for fallback percent-change questions.
Table~\ref{tab:retrieval-portfolio-ablation} makes the tradeoff explicit: the conservative v44 selector has no observed paired losses but leaves many hybrid-only wins unused, the v45 confidence selector crosses the 0.40 open-retrieval line with one paired loss, and the guarded v46 selector preserves the same rounded exact match while removing that loss through a complete-year-coverage guard.

\paragraph{Retrieval coverage diagnostics.}
The v28 manifest now emits retrieval diagnostics for every retrieval setting.
On FinQA-300 Open BM25, the gold source appears in the retrieved top-\(8\) for 269/300 examples (0.897); among 145 errors, 115 still contain the gold source.
On FinQA-600 Open BM25, source hit@8 is 515/600 (0.858), and errors split into 302 wrong answers with source evidence retrieved versus 82 wrong answers without source evidence.
Thus many failures occur after source exposure, making operand grounding and verifier-guided repair central.

\paragraph{FinQA-600 stress subset.}
To avoid overfitting the narrative to the 300-example diagnostic subset, we also construct a deterministic 600-example FinQA validation subset using the same seed and a larger sampling pool.
Full EviGraph v43 obtains 0.503 exact match under oracle-document retrieval, 0.377 under open BM25, and 0.502 under source reranking.
Answer support remains substantially higher than exact match (0.820, 0.787, and 0.822), and operand-semantics checks remain high (0.970, 0.977, and 0.970), but supported wrong answers still expose unresolved operand ambiguity.
This pressure test shows that operand grounding, not arithmetic execution, is the main generalization bottleneck.

\section{Failure Analysis}

The manifest runner emits failure reports and row/operation diagnostics for every fixed run.
Table~\ref{tab:finqa-failure-categories} reports unresolved categories for FinQA-300, and Table~\ref{tab:finqa-row-operation-diagnostics} decomposes wrong numeric answers into numerator, denominator, year/period, row-label, operation-type, and ambiguous-supported-wrong signals.

The main lesson is that residual errors are not dominated by arithmetic.
Inspected repair rounds improved results by targeting small, named clusters: operand selection, percent-intent normalization, endpoint grounding, source-year compatibility, and narrow prose/table ratio cases.
These edits are kept only after fixed-manifest paired checks; the supplement gives the full failure-to-fix provenance.

The remaining bottleneck is evidence-state ambiguity.
On FinQA-300 Open BM25, 93 of 145 failed examples retrieve the gold source but do not expose the gold answer number in the top-\(8\) context, while only 30 miss the source entirely.
On FinQA-600 Open BM25, the corresponding split is 254 source-hit/gold-number-missing failures, 82 source-missing failures, and 48 source-hit/gold-number-present failures.
The dominant intents are percent-change and ratio-percent questions, suggesting that future gains should come from source-local evidence expansion, retrieval-aware operand repair, and stronger portfolio selection rather than broader arithmetic rules.

\section{Conclusion}

EviGraph-RAG reframes RAG as evidence-state control for auditable numerical QA.
It shows that the useful unit between retrieval and generation is not the retrieved prefix, but a compact state whose operands, operation, citations, and support can be checked.
The current implementation provides a reproducible ESO scaffold with FinQA/TAT-QA adapters, retrieval-setting separation, a table operation executor, verifier diagnostics, and failure-analysis tooling.
Future work should strengthen learned retrieval, operand grounding, and larger-scale benchmark validation.
